Figs & Honey is a Salt Spring Island spa run by a practitioner with over twenty years in the field. Her website hadn't kept pace: an aging WordPress build that looked dated and, worse, had been quietly compromised. Spam links were being injected into her contact page without her knowledge.

![The previous Figs & Honey WordPress site](/images/work/figs-and-honey/old-site.webp)

Cleaning a hacked WordPress install is a gamble that it stays clean. We replaced the platform entirely instead: a static-rendered Next.js build has no plugin surface for the old problems to crawl back through.

## Nine pages, three integrations, one editor

The site covers everything the business actually does: treatments, weddings, retreats, coaching, gift certificates, an online shop, a journal, and contact. The interesting part is how much of it the owner operates herself.

![The treatments page with Square booking](/images/work/figs-and-honey/treatments.webp)

- **Booking** goes straight into Square Appointments. One calendar across every service, so double-bookings can't happen. The site links deep into specific services rather than dumping visitors at a generic booking page.
- **The shop** is a Keystatic collection: each product is an entry with a photo, description, price, and a Square checkout link. Adding a new balm is a CMS edit, not a developer request.
- **The journal** is a posts collection with the same editing flow, and Mailchimp signup grows her list from the site.
- **Forms** are spam-protected properly: honeypot plus Cloudflare Turnstile verified server-side, which matters on a site whose previous life involved injected spam.

This build carries the largest Keystatic schema I've shipped (a 673-line config), which taught me how to structure singletons and collections so the CMS editor reads in the same order as the page. Numbered section labels, `itemLabel` on every array so entries read by name, and seeded defaults so no field ever opens blank.

![The Figs & Honey online shop](/images/work/figs-and-honey/shop.webp)

## The result

figsandhoney.com runs fast and clean with none of the old platform's baggage: no plugins to patch, no compromised code, and forms that don't leak spam. The owner writes her own journal posts, stocks her own shop, and takes bookings through one calendar.
